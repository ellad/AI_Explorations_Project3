## Context of use

As the User, describe when and where you would use the app and what you need from it. Record important circumstances, assumptions, and limitations.

- I would check the app at my apartment in Austin, Texas, in the morning before getting ready and leaving around 9 a.m.
- I walk to two back-to-back classes in the same building, then have a 1-hour-45-minute break. I usually buy lunch on campus and eat outside when the weather is nice, then walk to another class at the bottom of a hill.
- My walk home takes about 20 minutes uphill. On Mondays I get home around 6:40 p.m. On Wednesdays I leave campus either after my last class at 3:30 p.m. or between 5 and 6 p.m. if I stay to work.
- My main concern is preparing for rain during the whole day away from home. Forgetting an umbrella leaves my clothes and shoes wet, and my shoes can slip on the campus's stone surfaces. Austin often feels hot to me, so comfort during outdoor walks also matters.
- Austin grounds my personal use case, but the app must support locations across the United States through manual entry and device location. My return time can vary, so the morning forecast needs to help me prepare for an uncertain schedule.

## User story

Write at least one user story grounded in your context of use:

> As a [type of user], I want to [need or goal], so that [reason or outcome].

Focus on the need rather than prescribing an interface or feature.

Approved user stories (needs beyond the personal context remain research hypotheses to investigate):

> As someone who walks around campus and may stay until evening, I want to prepare for rain before leaving home, so I can stay dry and choose footwear suited to wet campus paths throughout my day.

- **Other locations:** As someone planning a day in another US location, I want guidance for the weather there, so I can prepare for conditions that differ from those at home.
- **Accessibility:** As someone who uses a screen reader, I want to understand the forecast, clothing advice, and reminders without relying on the character illustration, so I can independently decide what to wear and bring.
- **Unfamiliar climate:** As someone unfamiliar with the local climate, I want to understand what the forecast means for clothing and rain protection, so I can prepare even when the temperature alone does not tell me what to expect.
- **Air quality:** As someone sensitive to poor air quality, I want to understand the air quality forecast for my location, so I can make informed decisions about when to spend time outdoors and whether to adjust my plans. **Scope note: integrating air-quality information is a stretch goal, not a core requirement of the brief.**

**Weather variables and recommendation evidence**

**NWS air-quality follow-up:** while NWS was under consideration as the weather provider, its live [OpenAPI schema](https://api.weather.gov/openapi.json) was checked; no structured AQI, ozone, or particulate concentration fields/endpoints were found. Air-quality alerts, where issued, would not substitute for routine AQI readings or forecasts. [EPA AirNow web services](https://docs.airnowapi.org/webservices) provide separate current AQI observations and forecasts by location. The [AirNow FAQ](https://docs.airnowapi.org/faq) documents API keys, endpoint-specific rate limits, generally hourly observations and daily forecasts. Forecast coverage varies by reporting area: one to six days, sometimes seasonal, and sometimes category-only. The service list flags older endpoints for retirement in fall 2026; use the replacement endpoints if selected. A backend/proxy and current browser-access checks remain to be investigated. AirNow integration is not approved for the core scope.

**NWS apparent-temperature follow-up:** the convenient `forecastHourly` resource supplies hourly forecast periods and regular temperature but not a dedicated apparent-temperature field. The raw seven-day `forecastGridData` resource includes apparent temperature; NWS defines it using wind chill in colder conditions and heat index in hotter conditions. The app can combine grid apparent-temperature values with hourly descriptions and icons. NWS documents hourly apparent-temperature data for the continental US and three-hour intervals in some areas outside it, so implementation must align time intervals rather than assume every value is hourly.

**UV provider follow-up — September 30, 2026:** NWS does not expose a UV-index field through the forecast resources reviewed. [Open-Meteo's forecast API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) provides hourly UV index and daily maximum UV index alongside seven-day weather forecasts without an API key. Its free access is non-commercial; the Developer confirmed that this prototype's actual use and deployment are non-commercial. [WeatherAPI documentation](https://www.weatherapi.com/docs/) includes hourly and daily UV index. Its [free plan](https://www.weatherapi.com/pricing.aspx) permits 100,000 monthly calls but only three forecast days; seven days requires a paid plan, and its API key should be protected by a backend/proxy rather than shipped in a static app. This comparison led to replacing NWS with Open-Meteo as the single weather provider.

**Technical and accessibility evidence**

- [GitHub Pages documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/what-is-github-pages): Pages serves static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Inference: a keyless API is simpler for this deployment; a secret-key provider needs a separately hosted backend/proxy to keep its key out of browser code. A build-time environment variable embedded in JavaScript does not hide a key. Framework choice is still open.
- [MDN Geolocation API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Geolocation_API): device location requires a secure context and user permission. Proposal: request it after an explicit action and keep manual city entry available for denial, timeout, or unavailable location.
- [MDN localStorage](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/localStorage): storage persists across browser sessions but may be blocked. Proposal: persist only the latest location, replace it on change, and continue without persistence if storage fails. Location queries still go to the provider; local storage does not mean location never leaves the device. Open-Meteo's [privacy terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms) say logs may include coordinates and are deleted after 90 days.
- [WCAG 2.2 reference](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/): supports text alternatives (1.1.1), information beyond color (1.4.1), text contrast (1.4.3), reflow (1.4.10), keyboard access (2.1.1), visible focus (2.4.7), and status messages (4.1.3). Proposal: readable outfit/reminder text alongside the character, labeled controls, keyboard date selection, and accessible loading/error updates. Reference screenshots alone establish none of these behaviors.
- Proposed app structure: normalize the provider response into one recommendation state, then derive character clothing, icons, text, and reminders from it. Choose outfit/reminder variants independently and retain selections when returning to a date. Keep transient selections in memory so persistent storage remains limited to the latest location. These are implementation proposals grounded in the brief, not API features.

## Decisions

- **Cost and use:** use a free weather service only where its terms permit the prototype's actual use. The Developer confirmed that the prototype and its deployment are non-commercial.
- **Weather provider — approved, revised September 30, 2026:** use Open-Meteo as the single live weather provider. It supplies keyless seven-day hourly forecasts including temperature, apparent temperature, precipitation probability and amount, weather codes, wind, and UV index, making it compatible with a static GitHub Pages deployment. Credit Open-Meteo in the information screen, disclose that forecast values are modeled, and re-evaluate licensing before any commercial or promotional reuse. NWS remains a researched alternative but is no longer selected because the reviewed forecast resources do not supply UV index.
- **Air quality — stretch goal:** integrating air-quality information is optional and outside the core scope. AirNow remains a possible source if this goal is pursued. Core weather and clothing recommendations must work independently of air-quality integration.
- **Additional feature — approved: occasion selector.** Everyday uses regular campus clothing; Game day uses burnt orange and white, with no school or color selector; Birthday party uses slightly dressier clothing. Occasion changes outfit style, while weather continues to determine suitable layers, rain protection, and reminders. Occasion alone does not imply indoor or outdoor exposure.
- **Style preference — approved:** offer Masculine, Feminine, and No preference clothing-style choices. Treat this as a garment-silhouette preference, not a question about the user's gender identity, and avoid gendering the character or the user in recommendation text. No preference is the default and draws from both compatible wardrobes rather than requiring a third duplicate wardrobe. Weather suitability and occasion continue to govern every result.
- **Selection reset behavior — approved:** each new app visit starts with Everyday and No preference. Occasion and style changes remain active during the current browser session but are not persisted across visits. This respects the brief's instruction to save only the most recent location on the device.
- **Occasion/style-selector presentation — tentative:** two clearly labeled three-option controls are one candidate, but their layout and control pattern are intentionally deferred until the Developer explores the required hand-drawn phone and laptop screens. The final design must keep both selectors discoverable, show their current values without relying on color, support keyboard operation, and provide comfortable phone touch targets.
- **Screen structure — approved:** use two primary screens. The main screen combines the character and outfit recommendation with location, selected date, a horizontally scrollable hourly forecast for that date, Masculine/Feminine/No preference selection, occasion selection, written recommendations, reminders, and weather-condition notices. The information and credits screen explains the temperature-category ranges and recommendation method; identifies Open-Meteo and the US Census Gazetteer data; states privacy and storage behavior; provides creator, evidence-source, artwork, and license credits; and explains how AI assisted the research, planning, site development, and any asset creation. The final disclosure must describe actual uses accurately rather than imply that AI made Developer decisions. Location selection may use an accessible dialog instead of a third primary screen. Loading, missing-data, permission-denial, and service-error states should appear in the relevant context and must be included in the specification and drawings.
- **Visual direction — approved:** use a playful editorial style with the illustrated character as the dominant focal point, a clean background, a bold but limited color palette, rounded forecast cards, and strong typographic hierarchy. Weather data and written guidance must remain easy to scan and must not compete with the outfit illustration. Final composition, colors, typography, and control placement will be refined through the Developer's hand-drawn phone and laptop explorations and documented in the specification.
- **Feature rationale and trade-off:** the WhetherWear reference demonstrates activity/style selection alongside weather advice. The Developer identified personal expression and campus social occasions as a useful direction, choosing these three styles. Their value for other users remains to be tested with peers. Reusing the character and garment components may reduce artwork, but the asset estimate must account for occasion styles and the brief's three outfit variations per recommendation category.
- **Recommendation categories — approved:** Hot, Warm, Temperate, Cool, Cold, and Freezing. Freezing adds coverage for lower temperatures across US locations rather than grouping them with all conditions below 50°F.
- **Forecast range — approved:** support today plus the following six days. This matches Open-Meteo's default seven-day forecast and gives users a manageable set of dates for advance planning.
- **Location input — approved:** offer device location and manual US location search. Manual search accepts either a city with its state or a ZIP code; users are not required to enter a street address. Ambiguous city results must include state labels so the user can choose the intended location. Save only the most recently selected location on the device, as required by the brief.
- **Manual location-search implementation — approved:** generate a compact local search index from current US Census place and ZIP Code Tabulation Area Gazetteer files, lazy-load it when manual search is opened, and send only the selected coordinates to Open-Meteo. Test duplicate city names, leading-zero ZIPs, representative states, Alaska, and Hawaii. If a ZIP is not represented, explain the limitation and offer city/state or device location. Credit the Census source in the information screen. Open-Meteo remains the only runtime weather-data service.
- **Location-index loading — approved:** split Census place data by state. For a submitted city/state search, load only the selected state's place file rather than downloading a national city index. Show a clear loading state and retry option, and keep device location available if the file cannot be loaded. Browser caching should make later searches for that state faster. Define a separate compact or sharded ZIP-index strategy in the specification.
- **ZIP-index loading — approved:** convert Census ZIP Code Tabulation Area records into ten local JSON shards based on the ZIP's first digit (`zip-0.json` through `zip-9.json`). Validate a submitted ZIP as exactly five digits, preserve it as a string so leading zeros remain intact, load only the matching shard, and look for an exact record before sending its representative coordinates to Open-Meteo. Cache the shard through normal browser behavior. If a ZIP is not represented, explain that it could not be found and offer city/state or device location. Test at least one ZIP from every shard, leading-zero ZIPs, and known unsupported special-purpose ZIPs.
- **Hourly forecast presentation — approved:** show a horizontally scrollable breakdown of all available hours for the selected date, similar in interaction to Apple Weather, including time, temperature, weather conditions, and precipitation probability when available. Keep one main outfit recommendation for the day rather than generating a separate outfit for every hour. Use the hourly data to explain changing conditions and provide time-specific reminders, such as bringing an umbrella for rain expected later in the day. For the current date, open the strip at the current hour while allowing users to scroll backward to earlier hours.
- **Main daily recommendation method — approved:** use the median hourly apparent temperature from 8 a.m. through 8 p.m. in the selected location's local time to choose the main temperature category. This prevents a single unusually cool or hot hour from controlling the outfit. When temperatures cross into another category for at least two consecutive hours, keep the main outfit and add adaptable, removable-layer guidance with the timing stated clearly. Rain and wind modify the recommendation independently. If apparent temperature is unavailable, the fallback behavior must be defined and disclosed in the specification.
- **Missing apparent-temperature fallback — approved:** use hourly air temperature for isolated missing apparent-temperature intervals so the app remains useful. Of the 13 hourly intervals from 8 a.m. through 8 p.m., at least seven must contain usable apparent or air-temperature data. With fewer than seven usable intervals, label the outfit recommendation as based on limited data instead of presenting it with normal confidence. Missing values must never be treated as zero or as favorable conditions.
- **Rain-protection reminder — approved:** within the 8 a.m.–8 p.m. recommendation window, trigger rain-protection guidance when any hour has at least a 40% precipitation probability, or when the provider reports measurable precipitation and probability is unavailable. State the relevant time or period rather than implying rain will last all day. Recommend an umbrella for ordinary rain, but use broader rain-protection or shelter language when thunderstorms or strong winds could make an umbrella inappropriate. The 40% cutoff is a product choice favoring preparation for the primary use case, not an official safety threshold.
- **Wet-weather footwear reminder — approved for testing:** trigger when forecast precipitation totals at least 0.10 inches from 8 a.m. through 8 p.m., or whenever freezing rain, sleet, or snow is forecast during that window. Recommend water-resistant or weather-appropriate footwear and caution on wet or icy surfaces without claiming that footwear prevents slipping. The 0.10-inch cutoff is a product hypothesis intended to avoid changing shoes for trace precipitation while responding to conditions likely to wet walking surfaces; peer testing may justify revising it.
- **Hydration reminder — approved:** trigger when any hourly apparent-temperature forecast from 8 a.m. through 8 p.m. reaches at least 80°F. One qualifying hourly interval is sufficient because the reminder helps a user bring water before outdoor walks; it does not assume the user will walk continuously for the full hour. Name the relevant time or period and suggest carrying water, using shade, and taking breaks without prescribing a fixed quantity or presenting 80°F as a universal boundary between safe and unsafe conditions.
- **Sun-protection reminder — approved:** trigger when any hourly UV-index forecast from 8 a.m. through 8 p.m. reaches 3 or higher, following EPA guidance. Name the relevant time or period and suggest a choice of sunscreen, shade, sunglasses, or protective clothing. Evaluate UV independently of temperature and cloud icons, and describe it as modeled forecast guidance rather than a precise personal exposure measurement.
- **Wind reminder — approved:** trigger when any hour from 8 a.m. through 8 p.m. forecasts sustained wind of at least 25 mph or gusts of at least 30 mph. Suggest securing loose items and choosing a close-fitting or wind-resistant layer. When wind and rain triggers overlap, replace ordinary umbrella advice with broader rain-protection or shelter guidance. The 25 mph sustained threshold aligns with the start of the NWS Beaufort strong-breeze range; the 30 mph gust threshold is a product choice for intermittent conditions and must not be presented as an official danger level.
- **Forecast-based weather notices — approved:** show prominent notices derived from Open-Meteo forecast conditions, not official government watches, warnings, or advisories. Notices may identify thunderstorms, freezing precipitation, snow, strong wind, or rain conditions already defined by the recommendation rules, state the relevant forecast period, and influence reminders such as replacing umbrella advice during thunderstorms or strong wind. Label the section “Weather notices” and state in the information screen that these notices are generated from forecast data and are not official emergency alerts.
