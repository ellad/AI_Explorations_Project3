About the project

About Cloud Closet

Cloud Closet turns a live weather forecast into clothing guidance for a day away from home.

How recommendations work

Cloud Closet looks at the 13 hourly forecasts from 8:00 a.m. through 8:00 p.m. in the selected location’s local time. It uses apparent temperature when available and falls back to air temperature for an isolated missing value. The median of the usable temperatures sets the main clothing category.

Temperature bands used for clothing recommendations
CategoryMedian temperatureClothing direction
Hot85°F and aboveLightweight clothing and minimal layers
Warm75°F to below 85°FShort sleeves and breathable clothing
Temperate65°F to below 75°FLight clothing with an optional layer
Cool50°F to below 65°FLong sleeves and a jacket
ColdAbove 32°F to below 50°FWarmer layers and outerwear
Freezing32°F and belowHeavier outerwear, a hat, and gloves

If another temperature category lasts for at least two consecutive hours, the app adds timed layering guidance without changing the main outfit. Fewer than seven usable hours produces a Limited forecast data label.

Reminders and limitations

Bring reminders are checked independently of the main temperature category:

  • Rain protection: precipitation probability reaches 40%, or measurable precipitation is forecast when probability is unavailable.
  • Wet-weather footwear: total precipitation reaches 0.10 inches, or freezing rain, sleet, or snow is forecast.
  • Hydration: apparent temperature reaches 80°F.
  • Sun protection: UV index reaches 3.
  • Wind preparation: sustained wind reaches 25 mph or gusts reach 30 mph.

Thunderstorms, freezing precipitation, snow, strong wind, and qualifying rain can also produce a forecast-based weather notice. In thunderstorms or strong wind, the app replaces ordinary umbrella advice with broader rain-protection or shelter language.

These temperature bands and reminder triggers are product rules, not personalized medical advice or official safety thresholds. Clothing needs vary by activity, health, exposure, and personal comfort. Cloud Closet’s notices are derived from forecast data and are not official watches, warnings, advisories, or emergency alerts. Check weather.gov and local authorities for official safety information.

Weather and location data

Forecast data is provided by Open-Meteo under CC BY 4.0. Open-Meteo combines numerical weather models; values are modeled estimates rather than guaranteed measurements from a nearby station. The free API has no uptime guarantee and is limited to non-commercial use. Commercial or promotional reuse requires reevaluating the Open-Meteo terms and service plan.

City and ZIP lookup data comes from the US Census Bureau’s 2026 Gazetteer Files and the 2020 ZCTA relationship files. Coordinates are representative geographic points suitable for an area forecast, not street locations. Census places do not include every locally used community name, and ZIP Code Tabulation Areas approximate USPS delivery areas, so some PO-box-only and organization-specific ZIP codes are unavailable. A ZIP label’s city is the Census place with the largest land-area overlap, not a guaranteed USPS preferred mailing city.

Privacy and data

Manual city and ZIP searches use files stored with this app, so typed search text is not sent to a geocoding service. Device location is requested only after you choose Use my location and your browser asks permission.

After a location is selected, its coordinates are sent directly from your browser to Open-Meteo for a fresh forecast. Open-Meteo states that troubleshooting logs may contain IP addresses and geographic coordinates and are deleted after 90 days; review its privacy terms for details.

Cloud Closet stores only the latest selected location—its label and representative coordinates—in this browser’s local storage. Search text, weather responses, device-location history, style, occasion, and outfit choices are not saved. You can remove the saved location by clearing this site’s browser data.
